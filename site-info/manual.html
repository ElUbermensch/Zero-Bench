The manual

Every screen, every field, and the handful of things that are not obvious. Written against the app as it is built, not as it was planned — the control names below are the ones you will actually see.

Your first hour

Nothing here needs an account and nothing needs a signal. You can do all of it in a car at the range.

  1. Add your firearm. More → Firearms & loads → + firearm. Name and chambering are the two that matter; barrel life and a starting round count are optional and drive the wear tracking.
  2. Add the load you shoot. Same screen, + load. Bullet, powder, charge, OAL. If you loaded it yourself in Bench, import it instead — see what crosses between them — because an imported load carries its chronographed velocity and its BC, which is what the trajectory solver would rather start its fit from than a generic figure.
  3. Create a session. Sessions tab → + session. Fill in target, distance, position, firearm and load, and put a name in Range location — that field is not decoration, it is part of how your DOPE is filed.
  4. Log the string. + shot on the session, tap where each round went, and dial your sights on screen as you dial them on the rifle.
  5. Read it back. Score, X count, extreme spread, mean radius and the group centre are computed as you go. Nothing to add up.
The one step people miss

Move the sight-adjustment pad. A session where the dial never leaves E0.00 / W0.00 records no zero: it still appears in DOPE, labelled no dial logged, but it contributes nothing, and the trajectory solver skips it entirely. See how a zero is recorded — this catches almost everyone once.

What needs a signal

Both apps install to the home screen and both work with the phone in airplane mode. The distinction below is worth knowing before a match, because the parts that need a signal are the parts that will quietly do nothing on a range with no bars.

Works offlineNeeds a connection
Everything in the shot record: sessions, shots, scoring, group statistics, matches, DOPE, the trajectory solver, targets, analytics, firearms and loads, file backup and restore. All of Bench except sync: lots, recipes, batches, brass, annealing, labels, workups. Pair fire, the leaderboard, cloud sync, cloud backup, and importing loads from Bench. Everything else you do offline queues and goes up the next time you sync.

Only four things need an account: cloud sync, cloud backup, the leaderboard, and importing loads from Bench. Pair fire needs a connection but not an account — a guest identity is minted for you.

Naming

The trajectory solver is called Trajectory in the menu, not “solver”. Firearms and loads share one screen. There is no separate Firearms tab — if you have seen that written anywhere, it is out of date.

Firearms and loads

More → Firearms & loads

Adding a firearm

+ firearm opens New firearm: Name, Caliber / chambering, Barrel life (rds), Starting round count and Notes, then Save firearm. Only the name is required.

Fill in the chambering anyway. It is optional in the form and it is required for that firearm to reach the server — a firearm with no chambering is skipped by sync, and the sync summary will tell you it was skipped. Barrel life turns on a wear bar that warns at 85%.

Once a firearm has two sessions against it, its card grows a chart of group size against round count — the honest version of “is the barrel going?”

Adding a load

+ load gives you a name, an optional firearm to tie it to (default Any firearm), then Bullet, Powder, Charge (gr), OAL (in) and notes. Pick the load when you create a session and that load’s extreme spread and mean radius accumulate in a per-load table.

Why importing beats typing

A hand-typed load records what you loaded. A load imported from Bench carries the measured muzzle velocity and the bullet’s BC from the batch you actually chronographed — and those two numbers are what the trajectory solver starts its fit from. Without them it falls back to generic figures and says so on screen.

Logging a session

Sessions → + session

A session is one string, or one stage of a match, at one distance in one position. Splitting a day into several sessions is correct and is what makes the analytics work.

The fields

  • Name (optional) — free text, e.g. “200yd prone slow fire”.
  • Type — Score or Sight adjustment.
  • Position — prone, sitting, kneeling, standing/offhand for rifle; two-hand, strong-hand, weak-hand for pistol; bench/rested and unsupported besides.
  • Fire mode — Slow fire or Rapid fire. Rapid fire simplifies shot entry to a single tap per hit and hides the per-shot wind panel.
  • Target, Range (yd), Range location — the last one autocompletes from ranges you have used.
  • Firearm and Ammo load. A quarantined Bench batch appears here prefixed ⛔ and marked — quarantined, greyed out and unselectable.
  • Group into match — standalone, add to an existing match, or create one.
  • Conditions — wind speed, a draggable clock dial for direction, temperature, lighting.
  • Ammo & equipment — collapsed by default: lot number, ammo description, equipment notes.

Then Create session.

Four fields decide where your DOPE files

Firearm, range location, distance and position. Those four are the key your confirmed zeros are stored under, in both the DOPE tab and the trajectory solver. Leave the location blank and everything lands in one unnamed bucket; type the range name a different way each visit and you will get one entry per spelling. Capitalisation and extra spaces are forgiven; different words are not.

Recording temperature is worth the two taps. The solver reads it back to work out what air each zero was confirmed in, and a zero with no temperature is assumed to be standard conditions.

Logging a shot

open the session → + shot

The header shows which shot you are on — Shot 4, or Sighter B — and your current dial to its right. There are two ways to record where the round went, chosen with the toggle at the top.

Tap to place

Three steps, in order, each with its own chip: 1. HOLD, 2. CALL, 3. SHOT.

  • Hold — drag to trace your wobble. Release and it advances. Skip it if you are not tracking sight movement.
  • Call — tap where the sights were when the trigger broke.
  • Shot — tap where the round actually hit.

Re-tap to correct at any step; ← back, clear and skip → sit under the face, and a zoom stepper magnifies the target when the group is tight. A live readout gives the ring and clock position, the distance between your call and the impact, and the correction to centre in both MOA and clicks.

In a rapid-fire session this collapses to a single banner and one tap per hit.

Ring and clock

For transcribing a paper target after the fact. Pick the Ring, slide Placement within ring to say how far across that ring the hole sits, and set Direction by dragging the clock face or picking an hour and minute. A live preview shows where it lands.

Not offered on plates

Zone targets — the long-range faces and any plate you have drawn yourself — are tap-only, because a zone has no ring to slide within. On those the Log buttons stay disabled until you place the shot.

The rest of the shot card

  • Sight adjustment — arrows bump one click each, or use the four-click buttons. zero clears it; dope snaps back to the setting the app pulled from your DOPE. The +1↑ +1↓ +1← +1→ buttons move a full MOA — four clicks — at once. One click is ¼ MOA. This is the field that records your zero — see the next section.
  • Wind — flag-angle presets, speed chips, and a clock face for direction, read as the direction the wind comes from. Optionally record the hold you predicted, in MOA left or right, and the app scores your wind calls against your impacts over time.
  • Sighter — toggle. Sighters are excluded from score and group statistics but still count as rounds through the barrel.
  • Note — free text for the call you want to remember.

Finish with Log & next → or Log & done.

On the first shot of a session, if you have shot that firearm at that distance, in that position, at that range before, the dial is pre-filled from your last confirmed zero and the app tells you which date it came from.

Reading the string back

Open a session and everything below is computed for you. Group statistics need at least two record shots.

  • Score as N–nX, scored on the ring each hole touches.
  • Extreme spread and mean radius, in MOA and in inches, with a confidence band on the mean radius — a five-shot group is a small sample and the band says how small.
  • Score decomposition — how many points you lost to dispersion versus how many to the group sitting off centre, with a plain-English verdict. This is the difference between “shoot better” and “move your sights”.
  • Call versus impact — how far your calls were from your hits, and whether they were biased in one direction.
  • Sight drift — your dial traced across the string.
  • Shot inspector — expand any shot to see its own plot with the hold trace, the call and the impact.
  • ↑ Share — a plain-text summary you can paste anywhere.

Matches and courses of fire

Sessions → + match

A match groups sessions and aggregates them. Build one from a template and every stage is created for you, in firing order, with its target, distance, position and fire mode already set — you shoot the day and fill in the shots. Two templates ship: the National Match Course (50 shots) and a Regional / XTC (80 shots).

Sessions can also be attached to a match after the fact, or detached again.

DOPE, and how a confirmed zero is actually recorded

This is the part of the app most worth understanding, because it works by observation rather than by a button, and nothing on screen asks you to do it.

There is no “confirm zero” button

A confirmed zero is the sight setting on the last non-sighter shot of a session. That is the whole rule. You do not mark it, save it, or confirm it — you dial your sights on screen as you dial them on the rifle, and the setting you finished the string on becomes the zero for that firearm, at that range, at that distance, in that position.

The DOPE tab states the same rule on screen: confirmed zero = the last shot’s dialed sight setting, in MOA, up and right positive, ¼ MOA per click.

Two consequences follow, and both bite people:

  • A session where you never touched the dial records no zero. If every shot sits at E0.00 / W0.00, the session still appears in the DOPE tab — labelled no dial logged — but it contributes no confirmed zero, and the trajectory solver skips it entirely. If the solver has fewer anchors than you expected, this is usually why.
  • Only the most recent session per cell counts. One zero per firearm, range, distance and position — the newest. Older ones stay visible as history.

The DOPE tab files everything by firearm, then range, then distance and position, warns when a zero has walked three clicks or more between your oldest and newest sessions in a slot — once there are three of them — and prints a card with ⤓ card — sized to tape to a stock.

Trajectory — the solver

More → Trajectory

Every ballistics app asks for a muzzle velocity and a BC and draws you a curve. Both numbers are usually wrong: box velocity comes from a longer barrel than yours, and a published BC is a marketing figure. The curve is wrong by an amount you cannot see at 200 and cannot miss at 1000.

This one works from the other end. It takes the confirmed zeros you already have, runs a real drag model, and searches for the muzzle velocity and drag that make a trajectory pass through the distances you have actually fired. Then it carries that curve out to the ones you have not.

What it needs

  • Two confirmed zeros at least 100 yards apart, on the same firearm, in the same position, at the same range, shot with the same load. Three is better.
  • A fourth is what defends against a zero that is only a minute out. Three anchors against two unknowns leaves too little in the residuals for a small error to show. A gross mistype — a misplaced decimal point — is caught with three, and the screen names the zero that disagrees.
  • A load with real numbers, ideally imported from a Bench batch, so the fit starts from a chronographed velocity and a real BC rather than generic figures.

The fields

Beyond the firearm picker there are five, and there is deliberately no muzzle velocity or BC box — those come from the load.

  • A firearm picker, shown only if you have more than one.
  • Position and Range — each option says how many distances it can contribute, and the one with the most is chosen for you.
  • Temp °F and Station pressure inHg — today’s air.
  • Pressure when your zeros were shot — a session records its temperature but not its pressure, so this is how the fit learns what air your zeros were confirmed in. Left blank it assumes 29.92 and tells you it assumed.

Station pressure is the reading off a meter, not the sea-level figure a weather app shows. They differ by roughly an inch per thousand feet of elevation, and every inch is close to a minute at 1000 yards — so at a mountain range the gap between the two figures is several MOA.

Reading the answer

A green ✓ trued to your rifle means it found a fit, and it tells you the muzzle velocity and BC it settled on, how far each is from what it started with, and how closely the curve passes through your zeros. The Come-ups table then runs 100 to 1200 yards. Each row says whether that distance is confirmed, interpolated between your zeros, or how far past your furthest zero it is reaching — and flags transonic and subsonic, where a drag model does its worst work.

Below that, Between positions shows offsets it has learned from your own data: where you have shot two positions at the same distance and range, it pairs them and averages the difference. No table has that number for you, because it comes from your cheek weld and your sling.

When it refuses, and why that is the point

  • Not enough spread. Two zeros close together cannot separate velocity from drag — raise one and lower the other and the curve passes through the same points. It shows the load’s own numbers instead and says they are a starting point, not your rifle.
  • An impossible atmosphere. Temperature outside −60 to 140 °F or pressure outside 15 to 33 inHg stops the solve entirely rather than answering a question you did not ask. Typing 1013 — hectopascals, which is what most weather apps show — gets you a message naming the unit.
  • A zero that disagrees with the others. The fit checks its own residuals and names the anchor that does not fit rather than absorbing it.

Analytics

The Analytics tab is about trends rather than any one string.

  • Group size over time, bucketed by position and distance, with a flag on whether a change is real or inside the noise for that sample size.
  • Shot order — whether your first cold round goes somewhere different, and whether you walk during a string.
  • Correction response — when you dial a correction, does the group move by what you dialled? How often does it move the wrong way? This finds a scope that is not tracking, and it finds a shooter dialling the wrong direction under time.
  • Season points budget, across every session you have logged.
  • Classification pace against NRA High Power conventional bands, over your last 240 match-linked record shots, and only once you have fifty of them. It is labelled pace rather than classification, because it is your own arithmetic and not an official record.
  • Lifetime wind call accuracy.

Targets

More → Targets

Forty-two NRA faces ship built in: 11 High Power, 13 Smallbore, 13 Pistol, 3 F-Class and 2 Long Range. Every ring diameter is transcribed from the published dimensions rather than derived from a reduction formula — the formula is close and it is wrong. On the B‑19 it gives 1.80, 3.60, 5.40 and 7.20 inches where the rule book says 1.78, 3.58, 5.38 and 7.18 — two hundredths of an inch on every diameter, which is exactly the margin a shot on the line is scored by.

Pin the ones you shoot and they sit at the top of every picker, in your order. A High Power shooter needs three faces and will never open the other thirty-nine. You can also hide built-ins you never use, and restore them later.

Custom targets come in three flavours: Rings for a full ring specification, Quick rings for an evenly-spaced face, and Plate for steel and scoring zones.

Pair fire

Two phones on one string. A shooter’s shots mirror to everyone on the relay as they are logged, and each shooter’s string is drawn over the other’s target in their own colour. Needs a connection; does not need an account.

To host

Open your session, find the pair-fire card, put your name in and press ● go live. You get a four-character code — read it out. end closes the relay.

To shoot alongside someone

Open your own session, press join on its pair-fire card, and enter their code. This is the important distinction: joining from your own session is what makes your shots mirror.

To coach or watch

● join in the Sessions header, enter the code, and pick Coach. You get the full relay view: both strings, the roster, and a feed for wind calls and chatter. A coach scores nothing and cannot fire.

A hollow ring joined to an impact

That is the shooter’s call — where the sights were when the shot broke. The gap between the two is the shot they did not know they threw. It is the single most useful thing a coach sees on that screen.

Leaving a session does not end the relay; it ends when the host presses end, or it expires on its own. If the status flips to ● not mirroring, your own session is still recording — it is the mirror that has stopped, usually because the same account joined the relay from a second device.

The leaderboard

Opt-in per session, published from the session itself and read at the top of the Analytics tab. Ranked by score, then X count, then mean radius, filterable by position and distance, with your own row highlighted. Scores are self-reported: it is a scoreboard among people who know each other, not a verified record. Needs an account.

What a published entry contains

Your handle, the date, position, target, distance, shot count, score, X count, mean radius and extreme spread — and your account’s internal id, which is readable by anyone with an account. Only sessions you publish appear; everything else stays private. Publishing needs at least two record shots and a distance.

Bench — the four tabs

Bench is the loading room: what you have, what you made from it, and what that brass has been through. It records no shots of its own — those belong to the app where the trigger was pulled.

A sync chip sits in the header on every screen: Sign in, or ⇅ with a count of records waiting. Tapping it takes you to Cloud sync — it does not sync by itself.

Component lots

More → Inventory → + New component lot

Bullets, powder and primers. Record the Product, the Lot code, Quantity purchased, the Unit (each or pounds), Total cost ($) and Vendor. Bullets also take a weight and BC in G1 and G7 — enter the G7 if you have it, because that is what the trajectory solver wants.

A lot, not a product. A lot number is the thing that lets you explain a group that opened up in October.

Stock remaining is derived, never typed: it is what you bought, minus what your batches drew. Low and out-of-stock flags follow from that, and powder additionally reports how many more rounds of a given recipe it will make.

Brass, and identifying it

Brass → + New brass lot

Beyond the obvious — cartridge, headstamp, maker, case count — four fields do the real work:

  • Firings before now for brass that already has a history. Batches loaded in the app count themselves.
  • Expected firings, which drives the end-of-life warning and blocks loading a lot that is past it.
  • Anneal every (firings) — the app nags when the lot is that far past its last anneal. Zero turns it off.
  • Colour code — the marks you actually put on the cases, with a clash warning if another lot already uses that combination.

Firing average is a mean across the lot, with a spread warning when the cases in it have diverged. The detail screen also carries Remove cases with a reason, +1 outside firing, Log anneal with undo, and Retire lot.

Culling does not age the survivors

Remove three split cases from a hundred and the firing average of the remaining ninety-seven is unchanged — they have been fired exactly as often as they had been a moment ago. Later firings then measure against ninety-seven, because that is the lot that was actually in circulation.

Identify

The Identify tab is for a case in your hand. Tap the colours you can see in each marking position, leaving ? where you cannot tell, and it narrows to the matching lots while drawing the case as you go. Or use By serial — type or scan the serial off a label, check character and all.

Recipes

More → Recipes → + New recipe

A recipe is a load specification: cartridge, bullet, powder, primer, charge, COAL, CBTO. The bullet, powder and primer fields autocomplete from lots you own.

Load data source is required. You must cite where the load came from — a manual and edition, or a manufacturer’s published data. Published max charge (gr) is optional and turns on an over-max flag.

What this app will not do

It records load data. It never suggests, interpolates or extrapolates a charge, and it ships with none. Every number in it is one you entered and cited.

Batches

Ammo → + New batch

A batch is a recipe plus four lots — brass, bullet, powder, primer — a date and a round count. It gets a serial automatically. Everything else is measurement you can record if you take it: measured charge and its SD, COAL, CBTO, runout, shoulder bump, bushing, primer seating depth, press and storage.

As you type the round count, a live draw preview shows exactly what comes off the shelf. It refuses to over-draw: if you do not have the components, it tells you which lot is short, by how much, and how many rounds those lots will actually make. It also refuses a cartridge mismatch and brass that is at or past its expected life.

A batch can be quarantined and released. A quarantined batch appears in Zero’s ammo picker struck through and cannot be selected for a session — which is the point.

Firing a batch

There are two ways the round count comes down, and they meet in the middle.

From Bench

Open the batch and press Log range session: rounds fired, distance, average velocity, SD, ES, group size, temperature, pressure signs, notes. Save, and the batch stops being untested, the count drops, and the brass takes a firing.

From Zero

Shoot a Zero session whose ammo load is linked to that Bench batch. On the next sync the session, its group and its individual shots go up, Bench pulls them, recomputes what is left and pushes the corrected count straight back. You do not type the number twice.

The condition that catches people

Zero only sends a session to the server if its ammo load is linked to a Bench batch, it has at least two record shots, and it has a distance. A session shot with factory ammo, a hand-typed load, or no load selected never reaches the server at all — not the session, not the shots. It is still saved on your device and still counts in every local statistic; it simply does not cross to Bench.

Once a session has crossed, Bench draws the target and the whole string on the batch’s detail screen, in the order the shots were fired. That is the join the two apps exist for: this group, from this batch, from these component lots.

Workup and ladders

More → Recipes → Workup on a recipe’s row

Every batch on one recipe, side by side, with the axis chosen for you — charge weight or CBTO, whichever is what you actually varied. Each rung shows velocity, SD, ES, group and any pressure signs. It points out the flattest step and is explicit that this is an observation and not a recommendation.

Build a ladder creates the whole set of batches in one pass rather than making you enter them one at a time.

Labels

Print one or Print a sheet of 8, from any batch. A label carries the serial, a case diagram showing the marking scheme, and a QR code back to the record. Scan one with a phone camera that has never seen the app and you land on a page showing the serial with a button to look it up.

The Marking scheme screen under More is where you define what those marks mean: the positions on the case, which palette colours you use, and how many distinct codes the scheme can express.

What crosses between the two apps

This is the part most worth being precise about, because “they sync” is not true in the direction most people assume.

RecordZero → BenchBench → Zero
Firearmsyesyes
Range sessions, groups, shotsyes—
Batches (as importable loads)—on request
Component lots, recipes, brass—not yet

In words: firearms are the only record that syncs both ways. Shot data flows Zero to Bench. Batches come to Zero only when you import them, deliberately, from More → Loads from Bench — that is a read of one view, not a sync, and nothing is pushed by it. Component lots, recipes and brass go up to the server but do not come back down; each needs a defensible merge rule before it can, and cloud backup covers moving a whole bench to a new device meanwhile.

Two smaller rules explain most surprises. A firearm with no chambering is never sent. And Bench pushes are chained: a batch whose recipe could not sync cannot sync either, and a session whose batch could not sync waits on that. Anything in that state appears in the Not sent list on the Cloud sync screen, each row naming what is wrong with it.

Backup, restore, and second devices

Three separate things, and they behave differently on purpose.

WhatBehaviour
File export / importA file you keep. Importing one replaces this device’s data. No account needed, works offline.
Cloud backup / restoreA whole-device snapshot per account. Restore merges: it adds what the backup has and this device does not, and never deletes or overwrites. Needs an account.
Replace everything from the cloudExactly what it says, behind a confirmation that names what goes — two of them in Zero. This is how you make a second device match the first.
Per-record syncFirearms both ways, shot data to Bench. Not a way to move a whole log.
A merge cannot carry an edit or a deletion

Cloud restore is additive. If you correct a session on one device, that correction does not reach the other by restoring — the older copy is already there and a merge will not overwrite it. To make two devices agree exactly, use replace. Firearms are the exception, but not here: they carry modification times and deletion markers, so their edits and deletions cross on the per-record sync rather than on a restore.

Back up before you experiment. The nudge on the Sessions tab that says a backup needs attention is worth acting on.

Troubleshooting

“The solver ignores one of my sessions.”

Almost always the dial. If every shot in that session sat at E0.00 / W0.00, it records no zero — the session still shows in DOPE, marked no dial logged, but the solver skips it. Otherwise check that the session has a firearm, a distance, a position and a range location — all four are the filing key — and that the range name matches the spelling you used before.

“The solver says it is not trued.”

You need two confirmed zeros at least 100 yards apart on the same firearm, position and range. Closer than that cannot separate velocity from drag. The screen tells you what it has and what it needs.

“The come-up table vanished.”

One of the atmosphere fields is out of range and the app refuses to solve rather than answer with a number you did not mean. The commonest cause is typing 1013 into station pressure — that is hectopascals; in inches of mercury a firing point is between 15 and 33.

“My sessions are not reaching Bench.”

Only sessions whose load is linked to a Bench batch cross, and they need at least two record shots and a distance. Check the load is a Bench import rather than a hand-typed one.

“Bench says records are Not sent.”

Not an error, and not a queue. A record lands there when it cannot be expressed in the shared schema — a firearm with no cartridge, a recipe with no cited source. Each row says why; fix what it names and it goes on the next sync. Records are chained too, so a batch whose recipe could not sync cannot go either: fix the parent and the children follow. Records waiting because you are offline are not in this list at all — they sit in the queue, and the header chip counts them. Refused by the server is the different, worse list, and it has a Try again button.

“Pair fire will not connect.”

The relay needs a live connection at both ends — there is no offline queue for it, by design, since a mirrored shot that arrives ten minutes late is worse than one that never arrives. If the message mentions anonymous sign-in, that is a server setting rather than anything on the phone.

“Cloud backup says anonymous devices cannot back up.”

Using pair fire signs you in as a guest, and a guest identity has no account to back up to. Sign in with an email on More → Cloud sync and it will work.

“The app says it is out of storage.”

Take it seriously — it means the last change was not saved. Export a file backup immediately, then free space on the device.
